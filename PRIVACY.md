# Privacy policy

_Effective October 10, 2026._

Plexo collects nothing about you. It has no accounts, analytics, telemetry or ads, and it never sends your data, your downloads or how you use it to its developers or anyone else.

## What Plexo connects to

- **What you download.** Plexo connects to the links you give it, and any server they redirect to, over the networks you pick. Those servers see your IP address, as with any download.
- **Torrents.** When you add a magnet link or a `.torrent` file, Plexo joins the BitTorrent network for it: the peers, the DHT and any trackers the torrent names. Peers see your IP address, as with any torrent client.
- **Updates.** At launch and every few hours, Plexo asks GitHub for its newest release, and downloads it from GitHub when automatic updates are on. GitHub sees your IP address and Plexo's version ([GitHub's privacy statement](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement)). A copy from the Microsoft Store doesn't do this: the Store updates it.
- **The browser extension**, if you install it, talks only to Plexo on your own computer (`127.0.0.1`). It hands Plexo a download with your sign-in for that site (its cookies), which Plexo sends only to that download's own server. Plexo keeps them encrypted with your system's keychain, never in plain text (and doesn't save them at all without one), and deletes them once the download finishes.

## What Plexo keeps

Your settings, your download list and each download's progress, in Plexo's folder on your computer. Nothing leaves your computer. Uninstalling Plexo and deleting that folder removes them.

## The website

[getplexo.app](https://getplexo.app) uses no cookies or analytics. Your browser asks GitHub's API for the newest release, to show its downloads.
